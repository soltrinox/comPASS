# Enterprise governance narrative

## Individual-practitioner preamble (steal for the one-pager)

Lower cost at equal or better quality (easy turns stop going to expensive endpoints). Less time lost to under-provisioned hard turns (task class determines the model rather than habit). Continuity across machines. Freedom from single-vendor dependence — when a new endpoint appears, the graph evaluates it against *their* work in days instead of guessing from a leaderboard.

---

Enterprise buyers are not one persona. Three distinct buyers (prototype §6.2):

## Finance

- Spend attribution per project, team, and task class — replacing one opaque invoice
- **Enforced** budget envelopes (not after-the-fact reporting)
- Savings quantified against a documented single-model baseline (falsifiable claim)

## Security and compliance

- Data classification drives endpoint eligibility, **enforced at the routing layer**
- Auditable record of which endpoint received which context digest
- Local-only routing for regulated or classified work
- Residency-aware filtering
- Reduced concentration risk in a single external provider

## Engineering leadership

- Faster onboarding through portable project context (Pillar 5)
- Measured rather than anecdotal model decisions
- **Procurement leverage:** an organization that can demonstrate per-task equivalence between two vendors and switch with bounded effort negotiates from a materially different position. That leverage may exceed direct routing savings for a large buyer.

---

## What we do not say

No "we lock you in" language. Portability of the bundle format and the ability to stay on a local-only graph forever are features, not concessions. Governance is enforcement of *the buyer's* policy, not capture of their corpus.
